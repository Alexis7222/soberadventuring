How to Start Freelancing on Upwork With No Experience

By Alexis  ·  June 22, 2026  ·  6 min read

If you want to know how to start freelancing on Upwork with no experience, the honest answer is that the platform is harder than it looks but more accessible than most people assume. Upwork has over 18 million registered freelancers competing for work, which sounds discouraging until you realize that most of them never send a well-written, targeted proposal. What actually separates people who land clients quickly from those who give up after two weeks is usually not skill level — it is how they position themselves and how they approach the early stage.

How to Start Freelancing on Upwork With No Experience: Set Up Your Profile to Convert

Your Upwork profile functions like a landing page, and clients decide within a few seconds whether to read further or move on. The most common mistake new freelancers make is writing a profile that talks about themselves rather than about what a client gets. Your headline and overview should communicate a specific outcome you can deliver, not a list of adjectives describing your work ethic. Something like "I help small e-commerce brands write product descriptions that reduce return rates" is more effective than "Hardworking writer with attention to detail."

Even without paid client work, you can build credibility into your profile through relevant experience from previous jobs, volunteer work, or personal projects. Portfolio samples matter more than client reviews at this stage, and you can create them proactively: write a sample blog post in your niche, build a mock spreadsheet showing data analysis, or record a short video walkthrough of a process you know well. Upwork also allows you to take skill tests and earn certifications directly on the platform, which can signal competence when your review count is still at zero.

A complete profile with a professional headshot, a filled-out work history, and at least two portfolio items will rank higher in Upwork's search algorithm than an incomplete one. Upwork's own data shows that freelancers with complete profiles are significantly more likely to be hired, and the difference often comes down to sections that new users skip, like the specialized profile feature, which lets you create a second version of your profile targeting a different niche or service type.

Writing Proposals That Actually Get Read

The default approach most beginners take is to copy and paste a generic proposal and change the client's name. Clients who post jobs regularly can identify this in the first sentence, and those proposals are usually archived without a response. A proposal that works opens by referencing something specific about the job posting, demonstrates that you understand what the client actually needs, and offers a small proof of competence before asking for anything.

Shorter proposals almost always outperform longer ones. Three to five focused paragraphs are generally more effective than a ten-paragraph pitch that buries the key points. A useful structure is to acknowledge what the client described, briefly explain your relevant background or approach, and then ask one specific question that shows you have already thought about the problem. That question serves two purposes: it signals genuine interest, and it opens a conversation that can naturally lead to a hire.

Upwork uses a credit system called Connects to submit proposals, with most jobs costing between two and six Connects. New accounts receive a free allocation to start with, but you will go through them quickly if you apply broadly without a strategy. It is more effective to send ten well-targeted proposals than to spray forty generic ones. Filtering for jobs posted within the last 24 hours, preferably with fewer than five proposals already submitted, increases your chances of being seen before a client has already made up their mind.

Pricing When You Are Starting From Zero

New freelancers often assume they need to price as low as possible to compete, and while there is some truth to this in the early weeks, it is easy to underprice yourself in ways that attract the wrong clients. Clients who are specifically looking for the cheapest option available tend to be the most demanding and the least likely to leave a useful review. A more effective approach is to price slightly below mid-market for your skill area, frame your rate around the outcome you deliver, and focus on getting two or three strong reviews quickly rather than optimizing for hourly rate.

Upwork's own data suggests that the average hourly rate on the platform varies widely by category, ranging from around $15 per hour for entry-level data entry work to well above $100 per hour for specialized development or consulting. The rate you start at is not the rate you are locked into, and most freelancers raise their rates meaningfully after their first five to ten reviews. Treating the early stage as a trust-building phase rather than a permanent pricing position can make it easier to accept lower rates without feeling stuck.

Fixed-price contracts can be a useful entry point for new freelancers because they let you set a clear scope, deliver a defined output, and get paid and reviewed without the complexity of tracking hours. A well-scoped fixed-price project also makes it easier for a hesitant client to say yes, since they know exactly what they are committing to. If a project goes well, asking the client whether they have ongoing needs at the end of the contract is a natural way to move toward a longer-term relationship.

What to Do When You Are Not Getting Responses

A dry spell in the first two to four weeks is common on Upwork, and it does not necessarily mean your profile or proposals are failing. The platform's algorithm gives more visibility to freelancers who already have reviews, which creates a real catch-22 at the start. One way to work around this is to apply to smaller jobs from newer clients who have not yet established a preference for established freelancers, since those clients may be more open to someone earlier in their Upwork history.

It also helps to treat the first few weeks as a data collection period rather than a pass-or-fail test. If you send proposals and track which ones get viewed versus which ones generate responses, patterns tend to emerge. A proposal that gets opened but not answered may indicate a pricing issue or a weak close, while a proposal that is not even viewed might point to a timing or keyword problem. Upwork shows you whether a client has viewed your proposal, which gives you more feedback than most job application processes do.

Some freelancers find it useful to offer a short, low-cost discovery call or a small paid audit before proposing a full engagement. This lowers the client's risk and gives you a chance to demonstrate your thinking in real time, which can be more persuasive than any proposal text. It is not a strategy that works in every category, but for service-based work like consulting, copywriting, or strategy, it can shorten the trust-building phase considerably.

Building Momentum After Your First Client

The first hire is the hardest, and after that the platform tends to get progressively easier. A single five-star review changes how your profile is perceived, and two or three reviews can move you into a category where you are competing for different quality of work. This is worth keeping in mind during the early stage, because the goal is not necessarily to make meaningful money in the first month — it is to get the profile to a place where the algorithm and client perception both start working in your favor.

After completing a project, it is reasonable to ask a satisfied client to leave a review. Most clients who intend to leave one will do so within a few days of the contract closing, but a brief, polite message thanking them and noting that reviews help early-stage freelancers can prompt people who might otherwise forget. The review you receive is public, so it is also worth making sure each project ends cleanly with clear communication and a delivered result, since a lukewarm review is almost worse than none at a time when you have very few.

The freelancers who gain traction on Upwork fastest are usually the ones who treat it as a real business from day one, not as a side experiment. That means having a niche, writing proposals with intention, and following up on conversations rather than waiting passively. Upwork rewards consistency and signals of professionalism, and those things are available to anyone regardless of how much experience they are starting with.

Getting started on Upwork with no experience is genuinely possible, but it requires a clearer strategy than most people bring to it. A targeted profile, specific proposals, and a willingness to price for trust-building early on will get you further than any shortcut.
