Alexis Antonelli · My Story

How I got here
from there.

Not a highlight reel. The actual sequence of events.

I got sober again at 24. No fanfare, no treatment center — just a Tuesday where I decided I was done. What followed wasn't a clean upward arc. It was the slow, unglamorous process of trying to figure out who I was without the thing that had organized my entire personality.

By that point, I'd cycled through over 20 jobs. The gaps in my resume weren't explainable in a cover letter. Fired, quit, ghosted, burned out — whatever the reason, I'd accumulated a record that no hiring manager was going to overlook. And I had mounds of diversified experience across industries, but none of it was packaged in a way the traditional job market wanted.

"So I quit trying to fit into the structure that was rejecting me. I focused on what I could actually do, and studied like I was preparing for the hardest test of my life."

I found freelancing, and specifically Upwork. I built a profile around the skills that were actually marketable — sales systems, outreach, pipeline building — and I was amazed to find that I made $30k within my first 4 months. Not because I had the perfect background. Because I understood what the client actually needed and I could communicate it clearly.

Today I run Impello Agency, a sales agency with over 13 case studies. I also coach high-potential people — usually people who are a lot like I was — to get extremely clear on what they're genuinely good at, then build and execute a 60-day plan to start making money from it.

I do this from wherever I want to be. Right now, that's Thailand. Before this, other places. The location changes. The work — and the discipline behind it — stays consistent. That's not an accident. That's a system.

"Sobriety didn't fix everything. But it gave me access to myself — my actual thinking, my real capacity — for the first time in years."

I started Sober Adventuring because I couldn't find what I needed when I was starting over. The content that existed either treated sobriety like a clinical condition or a spiritual awakening. I needed someone to tell me how to actually rebuild — practically, step by step, without pretending it wasn't hard.

So that's what I try to be for the people who find this.

A note: Although I'm trauma-informed and have built frameworks around this work, I am not a licensed therapist. I'm a person who went through something difficult, figured out a path forward, and is sharing what actually worked. If you're in active crisis, please reach out to a qualified professional — I can point you toward resources.
