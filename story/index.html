Six years of tries
before one that stuck.

This is the story behind Sober Adventuring, and the reason it exists the way it does.

I started trying to get sober at eighteen, and what followed was six years of attempts that looked like progress from the outside, with stretches that ran from two years down to a few months each time. I knew the shape of a starting-over well before I understood why I kept arriving at one.

Like most people I know, adolescence wasn't easy, and my skin was what I can only describe as itchy. I was eventually diagnosed with Pathological Demand Avoidance, a behavioral profile on the autism spectrum characterized by an anxiety-driven need to avoid everyday demands, though the working label at the time was conduct disorder, and the treatment was medication. The Adderall prescription came at nine, and with it came my first real education about chemicals: that swallowing one could make you acceptable, and that the quiet in your brain was something you could just buy.

“I knew the shape of a starting-over well before I understood why I kept arriving at one.”

That pipeline moved faster than I understood at the time. By my mid-twenties I had been arrested and made attempts on my own life, with five totaled cars somewhere in the middle of all of it. I got sober for real at a treatment center tucked in the mountains of New Hampshire, where someone finally explained the disease model to me in a way that landed, and I stopped spending energy on shame I didn't need to be carrying.

I am lucky to be alive, and lucky enough to have the confidence to put this on the internet in hopes it reaches the right person.

Where I am now

Today I live in Thailand,
and I keep myself busy.

Teaching work reentry and freelance skills to people in recovery, because income and structure are the parts nobody thinks to build first, and they matter more than most things.

Writing a memoir alongside a recovery workbook, both of which are taking longer than expected and will probably be finished by the time you read this, or not.

I built Sober Adventuring because I needed something like it and it didn't exist the way I needed it to. I hope it reaches whoever it's supposed to reach.

A note: I am not a licensed therapist. I have direct experience in rehab and mental health spaces, and I've built structured frameworks around this work, but above everything I'm a person who went through something hard and figured out how to get to the other side of it. If you are in active crisis, please reach out to a qualified professional, and I am happy to point you toward resources.
