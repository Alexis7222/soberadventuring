How I got here
from there.

Not a highlight reel. The actual sequence of events.

I started trying to get sober at eighteen. What followed was about six years of attempts that looked like progress from the outside and felt like drowning from the inside: two years here, nine months there, six months, five months. I was lacking an understanding of why I kept ending up in the same place until I went to a rehab center tucked away in the mountains of New Hampshire, where I learned about the disease model alongside other explanations for why I was the way I am.

Like most, adolescence wasn't easy and my skin was itchy. I had what was then diagnosed as conduct disorder, but what I now understand was Pathological Demand Avoidance (PDA), a behavioral profile on the autism spectrum characterized by an intense, anxiety-driven need to avoid everyday demands.

Substances came early for me, with my Adderall prescription at just nine years old. My favorite game was seeing if I could get out of taking the pills, and with every attempt I was met with a knowing from those around me as they sternly questioned, often with a raised brow: "Did you take your medication?" I've met many along the road who experienced this early ADHD diagnosis and medication thing, and I'm curious whether they're also concerned by the subconscious imprint it leaves behind.

"If you don't swallow a mind-altering chemical, you won't be accepted."

I took prescribed Adderall for over ten years, learning to love the quietness it offered my mind and then the acceptance others showed me when I was placated. The pipeline from this to addiction came rather quickly, and naturally.

What came next resulted in 5+ totaled cars, arrests, and suicide attempts, all before the age of 24. I am lucky to be alive and lucky to have the confidence to put this on the internet, in hopes it reaches the right person.

Today I live in Thailand, where I keep myself busy with a range of things I didn't expect to be doing:

  • Teaching work reentry and freelance skills to people in recovery
  • Designing silly, fun art that I've recently started to sell (check out the merch)
  • Playing terribly at pool
  • Writing a memoir alongside a recovery workbook

I built Sober Adventuring because I needed something like this and it did not exist in the capacity I had needed. I hope you get as much joy out of this corner of the internet as I got by creating it.

A note: I am not a licensed therapist. I have direct experience in rehab and mental health spaces, I am trauma-informed, and I have built structured frameworks around this work. But I am, above everything, just a person who went through something hard and figured out how to get to the other side of it. If you are in an active crisis, please reach out to a qualified professional. I am happy to point you toward resources.
